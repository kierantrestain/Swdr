# Series and factual workspaces

The series register records appearances, explicit interactions, relationship changes, knowledge, events and arc milestones. The audience reveal follows the episode and exact manuscript line. Story order is a separate number so a flashback can appear earlier in story time while staying inside the selected audience scope.

Add characters and records in Working draft. Choose an existing screenplay scene and line for each item. SWDR stores an immutable copy of that document revision and advances the editing head. Opening a source returns to that retained line. Select an approved canon snapshot to read the frozen story register. Approving a new canon preserves earlier versions.

Season, episode and as-of controls scope the graph, ledger, dossier, matrix, continuity evidence and JSON export. The matrix reports co-presence from explicitly recorded appearances and lists interactions independently. Continuity marks simultaneous contradictory assertions and events whose cause follows them. A reviewed exception stores its reason against the exact conflicting records. Episodic reveal filtering uses recorded episode and source metadata. Writers control what they disclose in unrestricted prose fields.

In Editorial, organise an episode as timed segments with talking points, questions, counterarguments and visual cues. Add project-wide evidence and claims in Sources, then link them from segments. A claim or source correction retains the prior revision and requires a reason. The rundown calculates sequential start and end times. Visual cues can reference an existing project asset.

Issue outputs freezes the chosen document, segment revisions, claim revisions, evidence revisions and brief. Research dossier, host guide, producer rundown and graphics and asset register render reproducibly from the same issue. Download the manifest for stable segment, cue and source identifiers. Correcting a linked claim or source marks the affected issues for reissue while their original outputs remain available. A branded brief can capture client copy, deliverables and approval with a retained AV revision.

The fictional demo has 24 series episodes over two seasons, a flashback, relationship changes between episodes, separate co-presence and interactions, and an intentional reviewed continuity conflict. The factual demo has 24 outlines within 60–100 minutes and a shared claim in two pre-issued episodes. The commercial example links a branded brief to the retained sixty-second AV cut.

The application computes source and spoiler scoping from structured local records. It does not infer appearances from manuscript prose, verify external claim evidence automatically, or apply permissions to free-form text inside an already accessible authoring workspace.
